Getting Started

Up and running in minutes with your choice of installation.

1

Add the Repository

Click the button below to add Sonorium to your Home Assistant Add-on Store with one click.

Or manually: Settings → Add-ons → ⋮ → Repositories and add https://github.com/synssins/sonorium

2

Install & Start

Find "Sonorium" in the Add-on Store, click Install, then Start. The addon will appear in your Home Assistant sidebar.

Sonorium needs the Mosquitto broker add-on (Settings → Add-ons → Add-on Store), started with Start on boot on.

3

Set It Up

Open Sonorium and follow First steps below: turn on a speaker, make a channel and play a theme.

1

Create a Compose File

Sonorium runs on any Docker host, with or without Home Assistant. The image is ghcr.io/synssins/sonorium:latest.

services:
  sonorium:
    image: ghcr.io/synssins/sonorium:latest
    container_name: sonorium
    network_mode: host        # needed to find speakers on your network
    environment:
      - PUID=1000             # optional: owner of the files Sonorium writes
      - PGID=1000
      - TZ=Etc/UTC            # your time zone, for log times
    volumes:
      - ./sonorium/config:/config          # settings and channels
      - ./sonorium/media:/media/sonorium   # themes and audio
    restart: unless-stopped
2

Start It

Run docker compose up -d, then open http://<docker-host>:8008 in your browser.

Sonorium finds Google Cast, Sonos, DLNA, AirPlay, LinkPlay and HEOS speakers on your network. Add one the scan didn't find by its address, under Settings → Speakers.

3

Optional: Home Assistant and MQTT

Connect Home Assistant and MQTT under Settings → Connection to use Home Assistant's speakers and control Sonorium from your dashboard.

4

Set It Up

Open Sonorium and follow First steps below: turn on a speaker, make a channel and play a theme.

🚧 Alpha Release: The Windows app currently plays through your PC's audio output. Network streaming to Chromecast, DLNA, and AirPlay speakers is coming soon.

1

Download the App

Download Sonorium.exe from the Downloads page or GitHub Releases.

It's a single executable—no installation required. Just download and run.

2

Run Sonorium

Double-click Sonorium.exe to launch the app. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning since the app isn't signed—click More info → Run anyway.

3

Create Your First Theme

Go to Themes in the app. Click Create Theme, give it a name, then drag and drop your audio files to upload them.

You can also import theme zip files shared by the community.

4

Start Mixing

Select a theme, adjust the track mixer to fine-tune volumes and interval, then enjoy ambient soundscapes through your speakers.

First steps

Before you start

This guide takes you from a fresh install to sound playing on a speaker. It has four parts:

  1. Enabling speakers: tell Sonorium which speakers it may use.
  2. Creating a channel: a channel is one stream of sound, played on one or more speakers.
  3. Assigning speakers to the channel: choose where the channel plays.
  4. Loading a theme: choose what the channel plays, and start it.

Sonorium opens on the Channels page, so every part starts there. The theme used in this guide, A Rainy Day... Or is it?, comes with Sonorium.

Enabling speakers

Sonorium finds the speakers in your home (from Home Assistant, and on your network in the Docker version), but it only uses the ones you turn on. A speaker that is turned off never shows up when you build a channel, so this is the first thing to set up.

1: On the Channels page, click Settings in the menu on the left. The menu opens to show the settings pages.

2: Click Speakers.

The Speakers page lists every speaker Sonorium knows about, grouped by floor and area (for example "Ground Level · Office"). Each row shows:

  • The dot on the left: filled in when the speaker is online and can be reached.
  • Name: the speaker's name. Click it to rename the speaker in Sonorium.
  • Source: How the speaker is connected to Sonorium. HA means Home Assistant, Discovered means Sonorium found it on your network, Manual means it was added by hand. There may be more than one entry for this. Finally, a badge shows how Sonorium talks to it, such as Cast (Google Cast), Sonos, Linkplay, or Airplay.
  • Address: the speaker's network address or Home Assistant name.
  • Area: This is generally populated when the speaker is discovered in HA. It can be changed in Sonorium; changing it here does not change the area inside of HA. Channels can pick speakers by area.
  • Offset: makes this speaker louder or quieter than the channel's volume, for a speaker that plays louder or softer than the rest.
  • Test: plays a short, quiet chime on the speaker, so you can check you have the right one.
  • Use: the switch that turns the speaker on for Sonorium.

3: Click the Search speakers box and type part of the speaker's name. This guide uses a speaker called Office_C97A, so "Office" finds it. You can also narrow the list with All, Home Assistant, Discovered and Manual, or hide speakers that are offline.

4: Click the Use switch on the speaker's row to turn it on.

The switch is now on, and the count at the top of the area (here "3 of 4 on") goes up by one. The speaker is saved straight away; there is no Save button on this page.

If a speaker you expect isn't listed, click Rescan network to search again, or Add speaker to add it by its address.

Creating a channel

A channel is one stream of sound. It plays one theme (or one playlist) on the speakers you give it. You can have several channels at once, for example one for the office and one for the living room, each playing something different.

1: On the Channels page, click New Channel in the top right. The New Channel window opens.

The window has two halves. On the left: the channel's name, what it plays, and its volume. On the right: the speakers it plays on.

2: Click the Channel name box and type a name, for example "Office". If you leave it empty, Sonorium names it "Channel" followed by a number, for example "Channel 1".

Under Plays, Theme is already selected. Leave it there for this guide. (Playlist plays several themes one after another; see Sequences and Playlists.)

Keep the window open for the next part.

Assigning speakers to the channel

The right half of the New Channel window lists the speakers you turned on in Enabling speakers, grouped by floor and area.

3: Click the Search speakers box and type part of the speaker's name, for example "Office".

4: Tick the box next to the speaker.

The speaker now shows as a tag under the search box, and the bottom of the list reads "1 speaker selected". To play on more than one speaker, tick more boxes. Ticking a floor or an area selects every speaker in it. To remove a speaker, untick it, or click the × on its tag.

Keep the window open for the next part.

Loading a theme

A theme is the sound itself: a set of recordings Sonorium mixes together, such as rain, wind and thunder.

5: In the list of themes on the left, click A Rainy Day... Or is it?. The theme's name appears above the list, and a Theme preset box appears below it.

A preset is a saved version of the theme's mix: for example, the same storm with the thunder turned off, or with the rain louder. A theme can have several presets.

6: Click the Theme preset box. A list of the theme's presets opens.

7: Choose how the theme plays:

  • 7a: Default settings plays the theme as it is, with no preset.
  • 7b: the preset marked with a ★ is the theme's default preset. Sonorium picks it for you when you choose the theme, so you only need to change this if you want something else.

Presets marked with the sequence icon (a square, a triangle and a circle), such as Storm builds, are sequences: they play several presets one after another. See Sequences and Playlists.

8: Click Create Channel. The window closes and the new channel appears on the Channels page.

9: Click Play. Sonorium starts the theme on the channel's speakers. It fades in over a few seconds rather than starting at full volume.

To stop, click the same button again; it now reads Stop.

You can change the theme or preset at any time from the boxes on the channel, while it plays. The slider under them is the speakers' volume.
